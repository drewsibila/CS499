# Professional Self-Assessment

Throughout the Computer Science program, I have learned a lot about software development, databases, algorithms, testing, security, and problem solving. I have also gotten better at figuring things out when something does not work the first time. A lot of the projects I worked on had problems that I had to troubleshoot, and that helped me become more comfortable reading errors, testing changes, and fixing issues one step at a time.

My main goal is to work in software development. I like building programs and seeing how different parts of a project work together. I am also interested in cybersecurity and could see myself getting more involved with that later on. This program helped me understand that computer science has a lot of different areas, and I now have a better idea of what I enjoy working on the most.

One thing I learned is that computer science is not just about writing code. Communication and teamwork are also a big part of it. Developers have to be able to explain what they are doing, understand what other people need, and make choices that make sense for the project. Throughout the program, I had to explain my code, write about my design choices, and think about how another person would understand or use what I created.

I also got more experience with data structures and algorithms. I learned that you cannot always use the same solution for every problem. I worked with things like HashMaps, PriorityQueues, sorting, and searching. These projects helped me understand that some data structures are better for fast lookups while others are better for organizing or prioritizing information.

Software engineering is probably one of the areas where I improved the most. I have worked with Java, JavaScript, Node.js, Express, MongoDB, Mongoose, SQLite, JDBC, Git, GitHub, and other tools. I have learned how the user interface, logic, database, and testing all connect together. I also got a lot of experience going back to older projects and improving them instead of always starting over from the beginning.

Databases were another area where I learned a lot. I have worked with MongoDB and SQLite and learned how to store, retrieve, update, and manage data. I also worked with CRUD operations and database queries in other classes. This helped me understand how important it is to keep data organized and make sure the rest of the program can access it correctly.

Testing has also been a big part of my work. I used JUnit on several Java projects and learned that it is important to test more than just the parts that are supposed to work. I also tested invalid input, duplicate records, missing records, and other situations that could cause problems. This made me more confident in the final programs because I had a better way to prove that the changes were working.

Security is something I have also learned to pay more attention to. I used input validation, error handling, prepared statements, and defensive programming in my projects. These helped protect the programs from bad or unexpected data. I learned that security should be thought about while the program is being built instead of waiting until the very end.

Outside of the three artifacts in this portfolio, I also worked on projects involving object-oriented programming, software testing, graphics, databases, and secure coding. I also learned about newer technology like generative AI. I think AI can be useful for helping with things like explaining errors, checking code, and creating tests, but I also learned that you still have to review the results and make sure everything actually works.

The three artifacts in this portfolio show different parts of what I learned. My Travlr Getaways project shows my software design and engineering skills. My CS 320 project shows my understanding of algorithms and data structures. My CS 310 calculator shows my database skills by adding SQLite storage and calculation history to an existing program.

Together, these projects show that I can take an existing program, understand how it works, find problems, improve it, and test the final result. They also show that I can work with different programming languages, tools, databases, and testing methods depending on what the project needs.

Overall, I feel like I have made a lot of progress during the Computer Science program. I am more confident with coding, troubleshooting, testing, and working with databases than I was when I started. I still have more to learn, but I feel more prepared to start working in software development and keep building my skills as I gain more experience.
